Scope: every ✓ on /pricing
Method: static review of src/ + api/
Branch: main @ 3fb2317

Recommendation

Do not switch Stripe to Live yet.

Of the 30 ✓ features advertised across the four plans, 11 are not delivered as described and 6 more are advertised as plan-exclusive but are available to every plan, including Free. Separately, three defects in the payment lifecycle would produce customers who are charged and not upgraded, and a subscriber who cannot use the product at all.

The core analysis product is real and good — the report, the risks, the evidence, the playbook, the assistant all work. The problem is the gap between what the pricing page promises and what the code enforces, plus a handful of activation bugs. Both are fixable; neither is fixed today.

Payment lifecycle

You asked specifically whether payment correctly changes the plan, unlocks the right limits, and revokes access on cancellation or failure. Findings are ordered by how much damage each does on day one of Live.

01

A successful payment can leave the customer on Free

BLOCKER · api/stripe-webhook.ts · src/components/PaymentModal.tsx

Activation happens in the browser: after confirmPayment() the modal calls /api/activate-plan. If the tab is closed, the network drops, or the request fails, the upgrade never happens — and the customer has already been charged.

There is no server-side fallback, despite appearances. The webhook handles checkout.session.completed, but this flow uses subscriptions.create with Payment Elements, not Stripe Checkout — that event never fires. The invoice.payment_succeeded handler only acts when billing_reason === 'subscription_cycle', and the first invoice is subscription_create, so it skips the initial payment too.

Worse, the modal treats a failed activation call as success: on a network error it shows "You're on PRO!" with the comment "webhook will catch it." It won't. The customer sees a confirmation, has been billed, and is still on Free.

02

Anyone can grant themselves any plan

BLOCKER · api/activate-plan.ts · firestore.rules

/api/activate-plan has no authentication. It takes uid and plan straight from the request body and never checks that the payment it verifies belongs to that user or matches that plan's price. A Starter customer can replay their own succeeded payment with plan: "pro" and receive Pro for $9.

Independently, Firestore rules grant each user full write access to their own document. A signed-in user can set plan: "pro" and a future planExpiresAt from the browser console, and zero out their credit ledger, without touching Stripe at all. Every limit and every feature gate in the product is client-side over data the customer controls.

/api/expire-plan is likewise unauthenticated and accepts any uid — anyone who learns another user's ID can force a downgrade.

03

Nothing on the server checks a plan before spending your money

BLOCKER · api/analyze-decision.ts · api/challenge-ai.ts

Not one API route verifies a Firebase ID token. /api/analyze-decision accepts uploads from anyone, trusts a client-supplied header for the page limit, applies a fixed 10-file cap regardless of plan, and calls OpenAI. It never reads the user's plan or credit balance.

The practical consequence is that limits are suggestions. The commercial consequence is that your OpenAI spend is uncapped and unattributable — a scripted client can run reports indefinitely with no account at all.

04

There is no way to cancel, and the FAQ says there is

HIGH · src/pages/ProfilePage.tsx

The pricing FAQ reads: "Cancel anytime from your account settings with no questions asked." The profile page has no cancel control, no billing section and no link to a Stripe billing portal — no portal session endpoint exists. The only way a customer can stop being billed is to email you.

This is a consumer-protection exposure as much as a product gap, and it is the cheapest item on this list to fix: one Stripe Billing Portal endpoint and one button.

05

Failed payments keep full access for about five days

HIGH · api/stripe-webhook.ts

The webhook handles customer.subscription.deleted but not invoice.payment_failed or customer.subscription.updated. When a renewal fails, the subscription goes past_due and the app notices nothing: planExpiresAt was set to the last period end plus a 3-day buffer, and the client only calls expire-plan after a further 48-hour grace — and only if the user happens to open the app. Roughly five days of paid access after a failed charge, then a downgrade that depends on the customer visiting the site.

Cancellation itself is handled correctly: Stripe fires subscription.deleted at period end, and access is revoked then, which matches the "keep access until the end of your billing period" promise.

06

The upload screen quotes limits that no longer exist

MEDIUM · src/components/DecisionUpload.tsx · src/lib/userService.ts

Two stale limit tables survive alongside the real config. The uploader tells Starter users "50 pages" and Pro users "100 pages" when the config gives both unlimited, and tells everyone they may upload 10 documents regardless of plan. PLAN_LIMITS in userService.ts still claims 2 / 5 / 20 monthly reports and feeds the usage bar.

Harmless on its own, but it means a customer is shown three different sets of numbers for the same plan across the pricing page, the checkout modal and the product.

What has to be true before Live

In order. Items 1–4 are the ones that decide whether a customer can be charged for something they don't receive; the rest close the advertising gap.

  1. Disable the Business purchase path. Make the card a contact form or a mailto. Nobody should be able to put $149 through a button labelled "Contact Sales" for features that don't exist.
  2. Make activation server-authoritative. Handle invoice.payment_succeeded for subscription_create (or move to Checkout Sessions), and derive the plan from the Stripe price — never from the request body. Stop reporting success in the UI when activation failed.
  3. Lock down the write paths. Verify a Firebase ID token in activate-plan, expire-plan, analyze-decision and challenge-ai; change Firestore rules so plan, planExpiresAt and the credit ledger are server-write-only.
  4. Enforce plan limits on the server. Read the plan, check credits and maxDocs / maxPages before calling OpenAI, and debit after. The backend already has getPlanConfig() and computeReportCost() — today only the Stripe pricing helper uses that file.
  5. Gate the features the Free card marks ✕. Decision Playbook, Smart Skeptic Questions and export must be withheld from Free, or the ✕ marks have to come off the card.
  6. Resolve the Pro claims. Either implement Priority Processing / Faster Analysis / Complete Report Export, or remove those three lines. Consider listing "If I Were You", which Pro genuinely and exclusively delivers.
  7. Fix Business credits. credits: null currently resolves to zero. Give Business a real allocation before anyone can buy it.
  8. Ship real export, or rename it. Either generate a PDF, or fix the print stylesheet for .dr-content and call it "Print / Save as PDF" on the pricing page.
  9. Add self-serve cancellation. A Stripe Billing Portal session and a button in the profile, so the FAQ becomes true.
  10. Reconcile credit expiry wording. Either key the ledger to the billing period, or change the FAQ to say credits reset at calendar month-end.
  11. Delete the stale limit tables. Remove PAGE_LIMITS and PLAN_LIMITS so the product quotes the same numbers the pricing page does.
Items 1, 5, 6 and 7 are copy and configuration changes and could be done in an afternoon — that alone would make the pricing page honest. Items 2, 3 and 4 are the real engineering work and are what make the plans mean anything. Live payments are safe once 1–4 are done and the rest are either fixed or removed from the page.
